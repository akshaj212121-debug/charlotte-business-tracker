*Archive edition. This issue was written on Nov. 23, 2026, from public records dated September 2026, to fill in the months before the newsletter launched. Everything in it comes from filings and data the site already tracks; nothing was added from hindsight except where it says so.*

On the second-to-last day of September, [JELD-WEN](/companies/1674335) told investors it had reached a deal with the people it owes money to.

The Charlotte door and window maker filed an 8-K on Sept. 29 describing a "commitment and consent letter" with holders of about **94.5%** of its 4.875% senior notes due 2027 and about **72.2%** of its term loans due 2028. Under the plan, noteholders who backed the deal could swap their old notes for new first-lien secured notes due 2031 at full face value. Noteholders who didn’t would get 93 cents on the dollar. Term-loan lenders could exchange at par for the new notes or take **86 cents on the dollar** in cash. The deal also asked creditors to strip most of the protective covenants out of the old debt.

Why it matters: in its 2025 annual report, JELD-WEN’s shareholders’ equity had fallen to $92.2 million against $2.01 billion in liabilities ([our November story](/news/jeld-wen-loss-narrows-equity-thin) has the numbers). A company with that thin a cushion has less room to refinance on its own terms. The new notes rank ahead of the old debt and are backed by the company’s assets. *(Hindsight: the deal closed on Nov. 20. See [the October issue](/issues/2026-10) and [our story](/news/jeld-wen-closes-834-million-debt-exchange).)* [Read the Sept. 29 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1674335/000167433526000126/jeld-20260929.htm).

## A quiet month for results, a busy one for boardrooms

Only **two** North Carolina public companies filed a results 8-K (Item 2.02) in September. The next month, 25 did. September sits between earnings seasons, so the filings that do come in are mostly about people and money.

**Sixteen** companies filed an 8-K under Item 5.02, the item for executive and director changes. The biggest:

- **[Lowe’s](/companies/60667)** (Mooresville) named five executive vice presidents effective Sept. 1, covering Pro and Home Services, Stores, technology and AI, strategy, and marketing. The company said the moves were meant to "establish clearer accountability." [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/60667/000006066726000119/low-20260828.htm).
- **[Albemarle](/companies/915913)** (Charlotte) picked its next CEO. Ragnar Udd, chief commercial officer at the global mining company BHP, will succeed J. Kent Masters Jr. by Feb. 1, 2027, and Masters will become executive chair. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/915913/000114036126035623/ef20081522_8k.htm).
- **[Duke Energy](/companies/1326160)** (Charlotte) added Joyce Mullen, the retired CEO of Insight Enterprises, to its board and its audit committee. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326160/000110465926110588/tm2626114d1_8k.htm).

## Borrowing and refinancing

Companies used the lull to line up money:

- **[IQVIA](/companies/1478242)** (Durham) sold **$2 billion** of 6.375% notes due 2034 on Sept. 23. The filing says the money will pay off its 5% notes due in 2026 and part of its revolving credit line. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1478242/000119312526399616/d128109d8k.htm).
- **[Columbus McKinnon](/companies/1005229)** (Charlotte) refinanced **$1.45 billion** of term loans on Sept. 21 and cut its interest margin by half a percentage point. It lost $229.5 million in its fiscal year ended March 31. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1005229/000119312526397298/d166585d8k.htm).
- **[First Citizens BancShares](/companies/798941)** (Raleigh) created a new 7.5% preferred stock and sold 300,000 depositary shares in it, about $300 million at liquidation value. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/798941/000119312526390576/d505182d8k.htm).
- **[Sturm, Ruger](/companies/95029)** (Mayodan) went the other way and ended its shareholder rights plan on Sept. 16, almost a month before it was set to expire. A rights plan, often called a poison pill, makes a hostile takeover more expensive. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/95029/000117494726000877/form8k-36350_rgr.htm).

## Layoffs: one big notice, and a same-day closure

North Carolina employers filed **6 WARN notices covering 834 workers** in September.

| Company | Location | Type | Workers | Effective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MasterBrand Cabinets | Kinston (Lenoir) | Layoff | 391 | Nov. 27, 2026 |
| Regional Acceptance Corp. | Winterville (Pitt) | Closure | 156 | Feb. 28, 2027 |
| Bossong Hosiery Mills | Asheboro (Randolph) | Closure | 122 | Sept. 9, 2026 |
| Regional Acceptance Corp. | Greenville (Pitt) | Closure | 97 | July 31, 2027 |
| Comfort First Heating & Cooling | Sanford (Lee) | Layoff | 67 | Nov. 16, 2026 |
| Electrolux Consumer Products | Charlotte (Mecklenburg) | Closure | 1 | Nov. 9, 2026 |

MasterBrand’s notice alone was nearly half the month’s total. Bossong Hosiery’s notice is dated Sept. 9, the same day as the closure date it lists for 122 workers. Regional Acceptance’s two Pitt County closures together cover 253 workers, with the later one not taking effect until next summer. Source: [N.C. Department of Commerce WARN reports](https://www.commerce.nc.gov/data-tools-reports/labor-market-data-tools/workforce-warn-reports).

## Charlotte area

The Charlotte region was quiet on layoffs: the only September notice in Mecklenburg, Union or the six surrounding counties was Electrolux’s one-person closure. In the boardroom, four Mecklenburg-based public companies filed executive or director changes: Duke Energy, Albemarle, [Curtiss-Wright](/companies/26324) and [Satellogic](/companies/1874315). JELD-WEN’s debt deal was the month’s biggest local financial story.

## Federal money: the fiscal year’s final days

The federal government’s fiscal year ends Sept. 30, and agencies sign a lot of contracts in the last weeks. Among the largest new North Carolina contracts that started in late September:

- **Seqirus**: $42.8 million from Health and Human Services on Sept. 30 to formulate and fill an adjuvanted, cell-based H3N8 flu vaccine. [Award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_75A50126F61015_7505_75A50122D00004_7505).
- **Premier Healthcare Solutions** (Mecklenburg County): two HHS data-analysis contracts on Sept. 30, $11.3 million for fertility data and $10.0 million for maternal morbidity and mortality data. [Award 1](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_7571MN26C00062_7571_-NONE-_-NONE-), [award 2](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_7571PS26C00061_7571_-NONE-_-NONE-).
- **Research Triangle Institute**: $16.2 million from HHS on Sept. 25 for analysis of the ACO REACH and KCC programs. [Award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_75FCMC26F0166_7530_75FCMC19D0095_7530).
- **High Lonesome Timber**: received $5.0 million from the Agriculture Department on Sept. 24 for Hurricane Helene roadside debris mitigation to reduce fire fuel. [Award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_12363N26F4216_12C2_12363N26A4041_12C2).

Federal "obligations" are commitments to pay, not money already paid. The [October issue](/issues/2026-10) has the full-year totals.
