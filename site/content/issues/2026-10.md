*Archive edition. This issue was written on Nov. 23, 2026, from public records dated October 2026, to fill in the months before the newsletter launched. Where it mentions what happened later, it says so.*

[Liquidia](/companies/1819576) bought peace in October, and the price was steep.

On Oct. 13, the Morrisville company settled all of its litigation with United Therapeutics, including patent suits over three patents and a trade-secret and contract case in the North Carolina Business Court. Liquidia keeps selling its inhaled drug Yutrepia for pulmonary arterial hypertension. In exchange, it agreed to three things, according to its 8-K:

- Ask the FDA to **remove a second use**, pulmonary hypertension tied to interstitial lung disease, from Yutrepia’s label, and stop marketing the drug for it.
- Make a **net payment of $175.0 million** within 30 business days.
- Pay **another $145.4 million** in two equal installments, due by Jan. 31 and April 30, 2027.

That is **$320.4 million** in total, about twice Liquidia’s revenue for all of 2025 ($158.3 million). Sales are growing fast this year, which is what makes the deal affordable: the company booked $529.8 million in revenue in the first nine months of 2026.

*(Hindsight: Liquidia’s 10-Q, filed Nov. 10, recorded a net litigation settlement charge of $389.7 million in the third quarter. That turned a quarter with $225.3 million in revenue, four times the year before, into a $236.3 million net loss. [Our story](/news/liquidia-settlement-charge-third-quarter-loss) has the details.)* [Read the Oct. 14 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819576/000110465926115672/tm2627557d1_8k.htm).

Three things stand out:

- **Ingersoll Rand’s profit nearly tripled** on revenue growth of 5.6%. The Davidson company earned $669.9 million, 2.7 times the $244.1 million of a year earlier.
- **Bank of America sold more and kept less.** Revenue rose 3.2%; profit fell 16.8%. [Our story](/news/bank-of-america-revenue-up-profit-down-third-quarter) walks through it.
- **Three companies swung from a loss to a profit**: RXO, Fortrea and Bandwidth. Fortrea’s profit was $0.6 million, barely above zero, on slightly lower revenue.

## Leaving the stock market, by sale

Two North Carolina companies stopped being public in October, and neither was in trouble. [Qorvo](/companies/1604778) (Greensboro) filed an 8-K on Oct. 5 reporting a completed acquisition and a change in control, filed Form 25 to delist the same day, and deregistered with Form 15 on Oct. 15. [Simulations Plus](/companies/1023459) (Durham) followed the same path on Oct. 6 and Oct. 16. Separately, [SPAR Group](/companies/1004989) (Charlotte) filed its Form 25 delisting notice on Oct. 1.

No North Carolina public company filed a distress item, such as a bankruptcy, impairment or non-reliance notice, in September or October. That streak ended Nov. 3 with [Driven Brands](/news/driven-brands-item-4-02-non-reliance).

## Leadership and money moves

**Twenty-one** companies reported executive or director changes (Item 5.02) in October, the most of any month this fall.

- **[JELD-WEN](/companies/1674335)** (Charlotte): CFO Samantha Stoddard gave notice on Oct. 13 that she would resign effective Nov. 13 to take a job outside the building products industry. Chief accounting officer Jeffrey Embt became interim CFO. On Oct. 28 the company launched the debt exchange it had lined up in September (see [the September issue](/issues/2026-09)). [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1674335/000167433526000129/jeld-20261013.htm).
- **[Truist](/companies/92230)** (Charlotte): Dontá Wilson, head of consumer and small business banking, will leave Dec. 31. The 8-K says he gets the benefits for an involuntary separation not for cause. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/92230/000119312526423384/d186531d8k.htm).
- **[Bank of America](/companies/70858)** (Charlotte) expanded its board from 12 to 14 seats, adding Dow executive chairman James Fitterling and former KPMG International CEO William Thomas. Two days later it issued 80,000 shares of a new 7.2% preferred stock. [Board filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/70858/000007085826000494/bac-20261021.htm).
- **[Albemarle](/companies/915913)** (Charlotte) signed a new **$1.5 billion** unsecured revolving credit line that runs to 2031. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/915913/000091591326000124/alb-20261026.htm).
- **[Unifi](/companies/100726)** (Greensboro) closed the **$60.0 million** cash sale of industrial buildings and land in Yadkin County to affiliates of Enovum Data Centers. The filing says the money paid off all of its term loans, with the rest going to its revolving loans. [Filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/100726/000010072626000018/ufi-20261027.htm).

## Charlotte area

Toter’s Statesville closure was the region’s only October WARN notice. Seven of the twelve companies in the earnings table above are based in Mecklenburg County. Bank of America and Truist both made leadership moves, and JELD-WEN lost its CFO in the middle of a debt overhaul.

## Federal money: fiscal 2026 closes

Fiscal 2026 ended Sept. 30. North Carolina’s 100 largest federal contractors were committed **$6.42 billion** for the year, compared with $3.35 billion for the same companies in fiscal 2025. Two Thalle construction entities and Orano Federal Services account for most of the jump; the [November issue](/issues/2026-11) breaks it down, and the [federal money page](/federal-money) has every recipient.

The biggest new North Carolina contract to start in October was **$99.6 million** from the Agriculture Department to Peachtree Lumber Co. on Oct. 7, for a Hurricane Helene fuels-reduction project. [Award](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_12363N27C4000_12C2_-NONE-_-NONE-).
