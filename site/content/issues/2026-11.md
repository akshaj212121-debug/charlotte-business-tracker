Truist Financial’s revenue rose 53% last year, to $20.32 billion. Almost none of that is growth.

Look at the two pieces a bank’s revenue is built from. [Truist’s](/companies/92230) net interest income was $14.09 billion in 2024 and $14.42 billion in 2025, essentially flat. Its noninterest income was the line that moved: **negative $0.81 billion** in 2024, then $5.90 billion in 2025. A single bad year in 2024 makes the comparison look like a boom. If you see "Truist revenue up 53%" anywhere this month, that is the context it is missing.

(Banks don’t tag one total revenue line in their structured filings, so this site adds net interest income and noninterest income together. The company page says so.)

JELD-WEN’s revenue also fell 14.9%. [Krispy Kreme](/companies/1857154) lost $515.8 million while revenue slipped 8.6% to $1.52 billion.

On the growth side, [Liquidia](/companies/1819576) went from roughly $14 million in revenue to $158.3 million, about elevenfold, and still posted a $68.9 million loss. [BioCryst](/companies/882796) nearly doubled revenue to $874.8 million and earned $263.9 million. Then, on June 29, it filed an 8-K under Item 2.05, the item for exit and restructuring costs, and amended it on Aug. 4. A company can be growing and cutting at the same time, and BioCryst’s filings show both. [Magnera](/companies/41719), which the SEC still lists under its former name Glatfelter, grew revenue 46.5% to $3.20 billion and lost $159.0 million.

## Filings worth reading

Seventy-four North Carolina companies filed a results 8-K (Item 2.02) since Oct. 1. That is third-quarter earnings season. Profiles on this site use annual 10-K figures, so those quarterly numbers aren’t in the tables yet. A few non-routine filings stood out:

- **[Driven Brands](/companies/1804745)** (Charlotte) filed an 8-K on Nov. 3 with Item 4.02: previously issued financial statements should no longer be relied on. It filed that alongside its earnings release. In June it reported an exchange delisting notice. [Read the Nov. 3 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1804745/000162828026069090/drvn-20261103.htm).
- **[Pelthos Therapeutics](/companies/1919246)** filed an Item 4.02 non-reliance notice on Aug. 13.
- **[Insteel Industries](/companies/764401)** (Mount Airy) reported exit or restructuring costs on Aug. 21, then filed its 10-K on Oct. 29.
- **Delisting notices (Item 3.01):** [Fathom Holdings](/companies/1753162) in May and again in August, [Humacyte](/companies/1818382) on July 31, and [SPAR Group](/companies/1004989) in July. SPAR’s Form 25 removal notice followed on Oct. 1, and it still filed a 10-Q on Nov. 13.

Two exits were deals, not distress. [Qorvo](/companies/1604778) and [Simulations Plus](/companies/1023459) each filed 8-Ks in early October reporting a completed acquisition and a change in control, then deregistered with Form 15 by mid-month. This site does not count those delistings as warning signs.

## Federal money: one contractor’s sixfold year

In federal fiscal 2026 (October 2025 through September 2026), the 100 largest contract recipients with a North Carolina address were obligated **$6.42 billion**. An obligation is money the government committed that year, not money already paid, so a single large award can swing a company’s total.

[Thalle Construction Co.](https://www.usaspending.gov/recipient/880ef35a-f087-3ed6-ac27-9d2211947165-C/latest) led with **$1.92 billion**, up from $285.4 million the year before. A related entity, Thalle-Bauer LLC, received another $379.0 million. [Orano Federal Services](https://www.usaspending.gov/recipient/672134e5-1420-82f2-0227-283f296d94c6-C/latest) came in second at $899.8 million after not ranking in the prior year’s top 100. Research Triangle Institute was steady at $381.9 million, up 5.6%.

The largest new award of the past 60 days went to [Peachtree Lumber Company](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_12363N27C4000_12C2_-NONE-_-NONE-): $99.6 million from the Department of Agriculture, under a contract whose description begins "Buck Helene fuels reduction." Next was [Seqirus](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_75A50126F61015_7505_75A50122D00004_7505) at $42.8 million from Health and Human Services, described as "formulation and filling of MF59-adjuvanted cell-based A/Henan." The [full list](/contracts) is on the contracts page.

## Layoffs: twelve closures since September

North Carolina has logged **71 WARN notices covering 6,955 workers** so far in 2026. Since Sept. 1 alone there have been 15 notices and 1,525 workers, and 12 of the 15 are full closures, not partial layoffs.

| Employer | Location | Workers | Type | Effective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MasterBrand Cabinets | Kinston (Lenoir) | 391 | Layoff | Nov. 27, 2026 |
| Foster | Winston-Salem (Forsyth) | 169 | Closure | Mar. 5, 2027 |
| Regional Acceptance Corp. | Winterville (Pitt) | 156 | Closure | Feb. 28, 2027 |
| Pella Corp. | Reidsville (Rockingham) | 133 | Closure | Jan. 31, 2027 |
| Bossong Hosiery Mills | Asheboro (Randolph) | 122 | Closure | Sept. 9, 2026 |
| Sealy Mattress Manufacturing | Trinity (Randolph) | 114 | Closure | Dec. 31, 2026 |

Regional Acceptance filed a second closure for Greenville (97 workers), and Toter filed two for Statesville (81 and 15). Randolph County had two plants announce closures 44 days apart.

Closer to Charlotte, Mecklenburg and Union counties account for 9 notices and 1,019 workers this year. The largest was Family Dollar’s closure in March, 373 workers.

The longest countdown on the list belongs to Mueller Systems in Cleveland, Rowan County: 70 jobs, effective March 31, 2028.
